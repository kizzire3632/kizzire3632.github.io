## 🎯 How to Use zod

zod is designed to be simple and intuitive. When you run it, you will see a clean and friendly interface. Here is a basic tour:

### The Main Screen
When zod opens, you will see a welcome screen. It will ask you to either create a new schema or open an existing one. A "schema" is just a set of rules that describe what your data should look like.

### Creating a Schema
Click "New Schema" to start. You will see a form with different options. You can add rules like:
- This must be a **number** between 1 and 100.
- This must be a **text** that is at least 5 characters long.
- This must be an **email address** in a valid format.

Add as many rules as you need. zod will remember them all.

### Testing Your Data
Once you set your rules, you can paste in some sample data. Click "Validate" (or press Enter), and zod will check the data against your rules. A green checkmark means everything is good. A red warning means something is wrong, and zod will tell you exactly what needs fixing.

### Copy and Export
When your rules are perfected, you can copy them to use in your own projects. zod generates clean, ready-to-use code for you. Just click "Copy Code" and paste it wherever you need.

Imagine you have a sign-up form on your website. You want to make sure that:
- The username is at least 3 characters long.
- The password is at least 8 characters with one number.
- The email looks like a real email address.

With zod, you set these rules once. Every time someone fills out your form, zod checks everything automatically. If a user makes a mistake, they see a friendly message telling them what to fix.

Suppose you run a weather app. Your app receives data from another computer (a server). That data tells you the temperature, humidity, and wind speed. But what if the server sends "hot" instead of the number 35? That would break your app.

With zod, you can say, "Temperature must be a number between -50 and 50 degrees." If the data comes in wrong, zod catches it before it causes any problems.

### Example 3: Configuration Files

Many applications use settings files. What if a user deletes an important line or writes a word instead of a number? Your app could crash. zod checks these files first. If anything is wrong, it gives a clear error message instead of a confusing crash.
